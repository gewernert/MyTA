# MyTA Design Contract

**Status:** Approved source for visual concept development  
**Version:** July 2026  
**Purpose:** Govern the creation of three complete MyTA website concepts before any production implementation.

## 1. Source-of-truth order

When sources conflict, use this order:

1. MyTA Website Execution Brief
2. MyTA Homepage Copy Lock
3. MyTA Final Reference Strategy and Persona Gates
4. MyTA Visual Evidence Board and captured references
5. This Design Contract
6. Current MyTA brand assets
7. The approved final design target
8. Codex implementation instructions

Codex is not allowed to override the approved target.

## 2. Website purpose

The homepage must create qualified conversations with university instructors who may be a fit for a focused one-course pilot.

It must also remain credible to:

1. Teaching and learning leaders
2. University leaders
3. Technology, privacy, security, accessibility, and procurement reviewers

The homepage presents the MyTA vision. It does not answer every possible objection.

## 3. Core product story

The page must communicate one connected assignment loop:

1. The instructor sets the assignment context and support rules.
2. The student works with guided support and remains responsible for the final response.
3. MyTA surfaces useful learning signals.
4. The instructor decides what to revisit, reinforce, or support.

The assignment is the unit of design.

## 4. Product evidence strategy

Current product screenshots are not required to begin concept development.

Use three neutral evidence frames:

1. Instructor assignment setup
2. Student guided assignment work
3. Instructor learning signals

These frames are placeholders only.

They must:

1. Preserve space for real product screenshots.
2. Show the intended role of each screen.
3. Use neutral wireframe content rather than invented features.
4. Avoid fake metrics, fake student names, fake testimonials, and fake outcomes.
5. Be replaceable without changing the page structure.
6. Use labels such as “Product screen placeholder” during internal concept review.
7. Never appear on the live website.

Recommended placeholder aspect ratios:

- Instructor setup: 16:10
- Student work: 16:10 desktop, 9:16 mobile crop
- Instructor signals: 16:10

The real product screens will replace the placeholders before final production approval.

## 5. Brand foundation

### Logo

Use the supplied transparent MyTA logo as the primary brand mark.

Do not redraw, simplify, add effects, or place it inside another decorative shape during concept development.

### Typography

Use DM Sans only.

### Brand colors

- Primary blue: `#2563EB`
- Secondary teal: `#16A085`
- Accent cyan: `#38BDF8`
- Dark neutral: `#172033`
- Light neutral: `#FAFAF7`
- Soft accent: `#EAF2FF`
- White: `#FFFFFF`

### Color roles

- Blue is the primary action and emphasis color.
- Teal supports secondary states and learning signals.
- Cyan is limited to small accents.
- Dark neutral is the default text color.
- Light neutral and white are the primary backgrounds.
- Dark surfaces are rare and contained.
- Gradients are prohibited unless the approved concept demonstrates a specific research-backed reason.

## 6. Visual direction

The website must feel:

1. Calm
2. Intelligent
3. Academic
4. Product-led
5. Contemporary
6. Purposeful
7. Credible to higher education

It must not feel:

1. Like a generic AI startup
2. Like a student project
3. Like a slide deck
4. Like a surveillance product
5. Like a broad LMS
6. Like a copied competitor
7. Like a template assembled from cards

## 7. Page grid

### Desktop target

- Artboard: 1440 pixels
- Primary content width: 1200 pixels
- Maximum wide media width: 1280 pixels
- Side margins: 80 to 120 pixels depending on section role
- Grid: 12 columns
- Gutter: 24 pixels

### Tablet target

- Artboard: 1024 pixels
- Side margins: 48 pixels
- Grid: 8 columns
- Gutter: 20 pixels

### Mobile target

- Artboard: 390 pixels
- Side margins: 20 pixels
- Grid: 4 columns
- Gutter: 16 pixels

No section may depend on an exact viewport height.

## 8. Typography hierarchy

Use DM Sans.

### Desktop

- Hero headline: 64 to 72 pixels, line-height 0.98 to 1.06
- Section headline: 42 to 52 pixels, line-height 1.04 to 1.12
- Subsection headline: 26 to 32 pixels
- Body large: 18 to 20 pixels
- Body standard: 16 to 18 pixels
- Supporting label: 14 to 16 pixels

### Mobile

- Hero headline: 42 to 48 pixels
- Section headline: 32 to 38 pixels
- Subsection headline: 22 to 26 pixels
- Body: 16 to 18 pixels
- Supporting label: 14 to 16 pixels

Rules:

1. Body copy must not be smaller than 16 pixels.
2. Hero headlines should not exceed four lines on mobile.
3. Section headlines should not exceed three lines on desktop when avoidable.
4. Paragraphs should usually remain between 45 and 75 characters per line.
5. Uppercase labels are optional and must not appear above every section.
6. Headings should communicate meaning, not decorate the page.

## 9. Spacing system

Use a consistent spacing scale:

`4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 80, 96, 128`

Recommended section spacing:

- Desktop: 96 to 128 pixels
- Tablet: 80 to 96 pixels
- Mobile: 64 to 80 pixels

Rules:

1. Do not create giant empty scroll regions.
2. Do not compress unrelated ideas into one visual block.
3. The product walkthrough may receive the greatest vertical space.
4. The institutional layer should be shorter than the product story.
5. The final CTA should feel conclusive, not oversized.

## 10. Surface and component rules

### Preferred

1. Open page composition
2. Large product evidence
3. Limited borders
4. Soft neutral backgrounds
5. Occasional contained surfaces
6. Clear alignment
7. One dominant visual idea per section

### Prohibited

1. Equal cards for every feature
2. Card grids used as the default layout
3. Heavy border systems
4. Full-width dark bands between every section
5. Small product screenshots inside decorative frames
6. Repeated floating browser windows
7. Sparkles, neural nodes, glowing orbs, and generic AI symbols
8. Random gradients
9. Glassmorphism
10. Excessive pills and badges
11. Saturated full-width blue CTA sections
12. Decorative motion with no product purpose

## 11. Signature MyTA visual idea

The concepts must explore one restrained connected-path idea representing the assignment learning loop:

`Instructor intent → Student work → Learning signals → Instructor action`

Rules:

1. It must function as structure, not decoration.
2. It may guide the eye between product states.
3. It may appear in the problem bridge or walkthrough.
4. It must not become a large infographic.
5. It must remain understandable without animation.
6. It must not resemble a neural network, orbit, or generic AI diagram.
7. It should use the brand blue and teal sparingly.

Each concept must interpret this idea differently while preserving the same product story.

## 12. Homepage architecture

### Section 1: Hero

Job:

1. Explain what MyTA is.
2. Lead with instructor value.
3. Show product evidence.
4. Present Request a Pilot and Watch the Demo.

Requirements:

1. Real or placeholder product evidence appears in the first viewport or directly below it.
2. No statistics, testimonials, or logo wall.
3. One dominant product visual.
4. Headline and product evidence must feel connected.

### Section 2: Problem bridge

Job:

Explain why the final submission no longer tells the full learning story.

Requirements:

1. Show assignment, hidden learning activity, and final submission.
2. Frame the middle as learning activity, not surveillance evidence.
3. Keep copy concise.
4. Transition directly into the MyTA loop.

### Section 3: Product walkthrough

Job:

Explain the complete assignment workflow.

Stages:

1. Set the assignment context.
2. Guide the work, not the answer.
3. See where support is needed.

Requirements:

1. This is the visual center of the homepage.
2. Each stage includes one large evidence frame.
3. Stages feel connected, not like separate feature cards.
4. The instructor, student, and return-to-instructor sequence is unmistakable.
5. Mobile uses a clear vertical sequence.

### Section 4: Why MyTA and how it fits

Job:

Explain the assignment-centered difference and the practical adoption path.

Requirements:

1. Do not use a large competitor matrix.
2. Explain the product’s center of gravity.
3. Show instructor control once, convincingly.
4. State that MyTA is intended to work alongside LMS workflows.
5. Include a contained institutional-review statement.
6. Keep this section visually secondary to the walkthrough.

### Section 5: Pilot CTA

Job:

Invite a qualified visitor to explore one assignment or course.

Requirements:

1. Primary action: Request a Pilot
2. Secondary action: Watch the Demo
3. Ask for one assignment and its learning goal.
4. Avoid enterprise procurement language.
5. Keep the CTA contained and calm.

## 13. Responsive behavior

Mobile is a separate composition, not a stacked desktop page.

Rules:

1. Preserve message hierarchy.
2. Put the most important product evidence close to the relevant copy.
3. Avoid horizontal carousels for core content.
4. Do not hide essential product stages behind tabs.
5. Product frames may crop to the most meaningful region.
6. The primary CTA remains visible without dominating the entire screen.
7. Navigation must remain simple.
8. All content must reflow at 200 percent text zoom.
9. Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences.
10. Controls must be keyboard accessible.

### Concept B: Editorial Academic Product

Primary construction references:

### Concept C: Precision Course Workflow

Primary construction references:

1. Class Companion product clarity
2. Gradescope adoption clarity
3. IgniteAI trust hierarchy

Rules:

## 15. Persona acceptance gates

Every concept must pass:

1. University instructor
2. Teaching and learning leader
3. University leader
4. Technology and institutional reviewer
5. Early-stage B2B marketing consultant
6. Professional product and web designer
7. MyTA founder
8. Student advocate and accessibility reviewer

A concept fails when any persona identifies a material issue involving:

1. Product misunderstanding
2. Weak relevance
3. Unsupported claims
4. Generic category appearance
5. Surveillance framing
6. Inaccessible interaction
7. Weak conversion
8. Loss of MyTA’s vision

## 16. Design acceptance tests

### Ten-second test

A visitor should understand:

1. MyTA works inside assignments.
2. The instructor sets the context and support rules.
3. Students receive guided support.
4. Instructors receive useful learning signals.

### Logo-removal test

Removing the logo should not make the page feel interchangeable with another AI education company.

### Product-dominance test

The real or placeholder interface should have more visual authority than decorative elements.

### Thumbnail test

At a small scale, the page should still show:

1. Clear hierarchy
2. One dominant visual per section
3. Strong product presence
4. Controlled rhythm

### Mobile test

The mobile concept must feel intentionally composed rather than mechanically stacked.

## 17. Implementation boundary

After one concept is approved:

1. Replace internal placeholder frames with current product screenshots when available.
2. Create final desktop, tablet, and mobile targets.
3. Lock the target.
4. Give Codex one section at a time.
5. Compare each render against the target.
6. Review complete-page integration at fixed checkpoints.
7. Do not let Codex invent or approve visual decisions.

## 18. Final contract rule

Research defines the audience, product story, reference roles, constraints, successful patterns, and failure conditions.

The concepts explore how those rules become a MyTA-specific visual system.

The founder selects the final expression.

Codex implements the approved target.
