# MyTA Website Visual QA

Use one decision: PASS, REVISE, or REJECT.

## 1. Five second comprehension

Can a new visitor identify what MyTA does, who it serves, and the next action?

## 2. Product truth

Is every interface, workflow, claim, and status supported by current evidence?

## 3. Visual authorship

Does the section have one recognizable composition rather than a collection of generic components?

## 4. Hierarchy

Is the main message immediately dominant? Is supporting copy clearly secondary?

## 5. Product prominence

Is the interface evidence large and readable enough to prove the product?

## 6. Conversion purpose

Does the section answer a buyer question and move the visitor toward the pilot or demo?

## 7. Higher education credibility

Does the page feel appropriate for university instructors and decision makers?

## 8. Responsive quality

Does each viewport feel deliberately composed, with no overflow, clipping, tiny evidence, or collapsed desktop behavior?

## 9. Accessibility

Verify contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, heading order, touch targets, alternative text, reduced motion, and carousel behavior.

## 10. Interaction restraint

Does every animation have a communication purpose? Is it pausable and predictable?

## 11. Whole page rhythm

Does the section create a useful contrast with the previous section without feeling like another website?

## 12. Generic AI design risk

Reject feature grids, glowing decoration, invented dashboards, floating labels, fake metrics, generic AI copy, excessive cards, and purposeless whitespace.

## 13. Evidence quality

Use actual browser screenshots. Do not accept a written self review as proof.

## 14. Stop rule

A passing section is frozen. Do not continue polishing it without a measured defect.
