# MyTA Concept Review Scorecard

**Rule:** Every item is pass or fail. A concept with one material failure is not approved.

## 1. Ten-second comprehension

- [ ] An instructor can tell that MyTA works inside assignments.
- [ ] The instructor sets the context and support rules.
- [ ] Students receive guidance while doing the work.
- [ ] Useful learning signals return to the instructor.
- [ ] The primary action is Request a Pilot.

## 2. MyTA vision

- [ ] The design expresses that AI should strengthen the thinking a course is meant to develop.
- [ ] The assignment is the unit of design.
- [ ] The Pilot feels like the entry point to a larger vision.
- [ ] The page does not reduce MyTA to tutoring, analytics, integrity detection, or an LMS feature.

## 3. Product evidence

- [ ] Product placeholders are large and readable.
- [ ] Instructor setup, student work, and instructor signals are all visible.
- [ ] Placeholders do not invent features, people, metrics, or outcomes.
- [ ] Decorative visuals never outrank product evidence.
- [ ] Replacing placeholders with real screenshots will not require structural redesign.

## 4. Customer relevance

- [ ] The Hero speaks to an instructor evaluating a course.
- [ ] The website presents the vision without answering every possible objection.
- [ ] Instructor control is shown once and clearly.
- [ ] Students are treated as learners, not suspects.
- [ ] The course-level entry point feels manageable.

## 5. Differentiation

- [ ] The assignment-centered loop is unmistakable.
- [ ] The page does not rely on “AI-powered” as positioning.
- [ ] The page does not look interchangeable with Nectir, Canvas, Turnitin, SchoolAI, or a generic AI startup.
- [ ] The signature move is specific to the MyTA workflow.
- [ ] The design does not copy another company’s brand.

## 6. Professional design quality

- [ ] The complete page has one coherent concept.
- [ ] Hierarchy remains clear at thumbnail scale.
- [ ] There is one dominant visual idea per section.
- [ ] Product screens have sufficient scale.
- [ ] Spacing is intentional without giant empty scroll regions.
- [ ] Cards, borders, bands, pills, and badges are used sparingly.
- [ ] The page feels credible to higher education.
- [ ] The page does not look like a slide deck or student project.

## 7. Conversion

- [ ] Request a Pilot is the primary action.
- [ ] Watch the Demo is the clear secondary action.
- [ ] The final CTA asks for one assignment or course.
- [ ] The path does not feel like campus-wide procurement.
- [ ] The CTA is visually conclusive but not oversized.

## 8. Institutional confidence

- [ ] Product value appears before institutional detail.
- [ ] LMS-companion language is accurate.
- [ ] No unsupported compliance, integration, partnership, or outcome claims appear.
- [ ] Institutional review is acknowledged calmly.
- [ ] Trust content does not overpower the product story.

## 9. Mobile and accessibility

- [ ] Mobile is intentionally composed.
- [ ] Core content does not depend on tabs, carousels, or hover.
- [ ] Body text is at least 16 pixels.
- [ ] Product evidence remains meaningful on mobile.
- [ ] Headings wrap cleanly.
- [ ] Controls are keyboard accessible.
- [ ] Focus states are visible.
- [ ] Reduced motion is supported.
- [ ] Content reflows at 200 percent text zoom.

## 10. Persona approvals

- [ ] University instructor: pass
- [ ] Teaching and learning leader: pass
- [ ] University leader: pass
- [ ] Technology and institutional reviewer: pass
- [ ] Early-stage B2B marketing consultant: pass
- [ ] Professional product and web designer: pass
- [ ] MyTA founder: pass
- [ ] Student advocate and accessibility reviewer: pass

## Required review output

For every concept, record:

1. Material strengths
2. Material failures
3. Evidence supporting each judgment
4. Required changes
5. Final status: reject, revise, or candidate

Do not average scores. A serious failure cannot be cancelled out by unrelated strengths.
