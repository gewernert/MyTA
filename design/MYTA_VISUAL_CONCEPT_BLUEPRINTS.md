# MyTA Visual Concept Blueprints

**Status:** Required source before concept prototyping  
**Version:** July 2026  
**Purpose:** Define three research-traceable visual concepts so Codex implements layouts rather than inventing art direction.

## 1. Governing rule

These blueprints translate the approved MyTA strategy, copy, design contract, evidence board, and reference roles into three complete compositions.

Codex may implement these blueprints in an isolated concept directory. It may not:

1. Choose a concept.
2. Merge concepts.
3. reinterpret the product strategy.
4. rewrite locked copy.
5. redesign production files.
6. introduce an unapproved visual motif.

Each major choice below includes its evidence source.

## 2. Shared requirements

All concepts use:

1. DM Sans.
2. The approved MyTA palette.
3. The supplied logo without alteration.
4. The exact Homepage Copy Lock.
5. Three neutral product placeholders:
   - Instructor assignment setup
   - Student guided assignment work
   - Instructor learning signals
6. The same five homepage jobs:
   - Hero
   - Problem
   - Product workflow
   - Why MyTA and institutional fit
   - Pilot CTA
7. Desktop 1440, tablet 1024, and mobile 390 targets.
8. No testimonials, customer logos, fake metrics, fake students, fake outcomes, or unsupported integration claims.
9. No generic AI imagery, sparkles, neural networks, glassmorphism, random gradients, or repeated feature-card grids.
10. One dominant visual idea per section.

## 3. Concept A: Connected Assignment Loop

### Design thesis

Make the connected assignment loop the visible structure of the page.

### Evidence sources

1. Granola for a continuous lifecycle rather than disconnected cards.
2. Linear for dominant product scale and controlled hierarchy.
3. Google for Education for continuity between brand promise and product evidence.
4. Turnitin Clarity for assignment-level specificity.
5. MyTA’s core workflow: instructor intent to guided work to learning signals to instructor action.

### Signature move

A restrained path begins at the instructor setup frame, passes through the student work frame, returns to the instructor signals frame, and ends at a small instructor-action marker.

The path is structural, not decorative.

Rules:

1. Use a 1 to 2 pixel line.
2. Use blue for the active segment and teal only for the return signal.
3. Never use arrows, orbit rings, nodes, glow, or animated particles.
4. The path must remain understandable without animation.
5. Hide or simplify nonessential segments on mobile.

### Header

- Height: approximately 72 pixels.
- Logo left.
- Navigation centered or center-right.
- Request a Pilot right.
- White background.
- No floating pill container.
- No announcement bar.

### Hero

Desktop composition:

- 12-column grid.
- Copy occupies columns 1 to 5.
- Product frame occupies columns 6 to 12.
- Headline uses 64 to 68 pixels.
- Supporting copy remains under 560 pixels.
- Instructor setup placeholder is the dominant visual.
- The connected path begins inside the product frame and exits subtly below it.
- Primary and secondary actions sit under the supporting copy.

Mobile composition:

- Logo and compact navigation.
- Copy first.
- Actions second.
- Product frame third.
- Product frame crops to the instructor controls that establish course context.
- The path begins below the product frame rather than crossing the Hero copy.

### Problem bridge

Desktop composition:

- Open white or light-neutral section.
- Large statement on the left: “A polished submission no longer tells you enough.”
- On the right, a three-point horizontal sequence:
  1. Assignment set
  2. Learning happens
  3. Final response submitted
- The middle region contains six small textual signals placed along the path:
  attempts, questions, AI support, checks, revision, misconceptions.
- No boxes around each signal.

Mobile composition:

- Statement first.
- Vertical sequence below.
- Signals appear as a compact list inside the middle stage.
- No horizontal scrolling.

### Product walkthrough

Desktop composition:

- This is the longest and most visually important section.
- Left 4 columns: sticky stage label, headline, and short copy.
- Right 8 columns: large product frame.
- Three sequential stage panels:
  1. Instructor assignment setup
  2. Student guided assignment work
  3. Instructor learning signals
- The path continues between panels.
- Only one panel is visually dominant at a time.
- Stage transitions use space and path continuity, not full-width color bands.

Mobile composition:

- Stage label and copy above each product frame.
- Product frames appear immediately after their copy.
- Path becomes a simple vertical line in the left margin.
- No sticky behavior.

### Why MyTA and institutional fit

Desktop composition:

- Two-column editorial layout.
- Left: headline and one paragraph explaining the assignment-centered difference.
- Right: four short positioning statements separated by horizontal rules:
  - Your course context
  - Your rules for support
  - Active student thinking
  - Useful next steps
- Below, a slim light-neutral institutional row:
  - Works alongside existing LMS workflows
  - Start with one course
  - Review the fit before scaling
- No feature cards.

Mobile composition:

- Headline and paragraph first.
- Four statements stacked with rules.
- Institutional row becomes one contained text block.

### Pilot CTA

- Light neutral contained panel, not a saturated full-width band.
- Copy left, actions right on desktop.
- Stack on mobile.
- Logo may appear once at reduced scale.
- Primary action: Request a Pilot.
- Secondary action: Watch the Demo.
- Final sentence: Start with one course before considering anything broader.

### Persona risk checks

Reject this concept if:

1. The path looks like a neural network or decorative infographic.
2. Product frames become secondary to the path.
3. The sticky walkthrough creates excessive empty scrolling.
4. The page feels like a process-monitoring product.
5. The institutional row looks like fake enterprise proof.

## 4. Concept B: Editorial Academic Product

### Design thesis

Present MyTA as a serious higher-education product with an editorial hierarchy and large, calm product evidence.

### Evidence sources

1. Google for Education for education-first purpose and human tone.
2. OpenAI Education for institutional information hierarchy.
3. Linear for restraint and product authority.
4. First Round’s guidance to combine clear product comprehension with an opinionated point of view.
5. MyTA’s point of view: AI should strengthen the thinking a course is meant to develop.

### Signature move

A recurring editorial margin marker identifies the four parts of the learning loop:

01 Context  
02 Work  
03 Signals  
04 Action

Rules:

1. Use numbers and a thin vertical rule, not cards.
2. Keep the markers small and functional.
3. Use them to orient the page, not decorate it.
4. On mobile, place the marker above each related section.

### Header

- Simple white header.
- Logo left.
- Three text links.
- Request a Pilot as a standard blue button.
- No floating container.

### Hero

Desktop composition:

- Centered or slightly left-offset headline above a full-width product frame.
- Maximum headline width: 900 pixels.
- Supporting copy width: 680 pixels.
- Actions directly below.
- Instructor setup placeholder spans approximately 1120 to 1200 pixels.
- The product frame begins before the first viewport ends.
- Warm light-neutral background behind the frame only, not the entire Hero.

Mobile composition:

- Left-aligned headline.
- Actions stacked or wrapped.
- Product frame below, cropped to the most meaningful setup controls.
- No centered long paragraphs.

### Problem bridge

Desktop composition:

- Editorial statement occupies approximately 7 columns.
- Supporting paragraph occupies 4 columns.
- A minimal line diagram below shows assignment, hidden middle, and final response.
- The phrase “the learning in between” receives emphasis through typography, not color effects.

Mobile composition:

- Statement, paragraph, then vertical line diagram.
- Keep the section short.

### Product walkthrough

Desktop composition:

- Three alternating editorial spreads.
- Stage 1: copy left, product right.
- Stage 2: product left, copy right.
- Stage 3: copy left, product right.
- Product frames remain large and use consistent framing.
- Numbered margin markers link the spreads.
- No equal card system.
- No decorative background changes between every stage.

Mobile composition:

- Marker, copy, product for every stage.
- Never place product before the stage heading.
- Stage 2 does not reverse order on mobile.

### Why MyTA and institutional fit

Desktop composition:

- One strong headline: “Built around the assignment, not another open-ended chat.”
- Below it, one concise comparison paragraph.
- Four positioning points run in a single horizontal row or two-by-two text layout, separated by rules rather than cards.
- Institutional content appears in a separate low-contrast section with a short adoption statement and no icon grid.

Mobile composition:

- Four points stack.
- Institutional content remains one short block.

### Pilot CTA

- Editorial closing on white.
- Large headline, short paragraph, two actions.
- A thin top rule signals conclusion.
- No colored background required.
- Keep the footer visually quiet.

### Persona risk checks

Reject this concept if:

1. It feels like a magazine with insufficient product clarity.
2. The full-width product frame is too small to read.
3. Institutional language appears before the workflow.
4. Number markers become decorative branding without meaning.
5. The page feels sterile or detached from teaching.

## 5. Concept C: Precision Course Workflow

### Design thesis

Make the website exceptionally clear and practical for an instructor evaluating one course, while retaining a professional higher-education tone.

### Evidence sources

1. Class Companion for immediate assignment-level comprehension.
2. Gradescope for reducing change anxiety.
3. IgniteAI for control, visibility, and adoption hierarchy.
4. Canvas Plus as a competitive boundary for signals and action.
5. Nectir as a competitive boundary against generic higher-education AI messaging.

### Signature move

A precise assignment-stage index remains visible within the walkthrough:

Set → Work → See → Act

Rules:

1. Use text labels and a restrained progress rule.
2. Do not use step cards.
3. Do not present it as a software setup wizard.
4. The sequence must emphasize learning, not task completion.

### Header

- Compact header with clear Pilot action.
- White background.
- No large navigation system.
- Demo remains a visible secondary action.

### Hero

Desktop composition:

- Split 5/7 layout.
- Copy left.
- Product workspace right.
- Product frame may include a narrow internal annotation strip identifying:
  - Course context
  - Support rules
  - Student work
  - Learning signals
- These are internal concept labels, not production copy.
- Headline remains the locked Hero headline.
- The product frame has more visual weight than the copy block.

Mobile composition:

- Copy, actions, then product.
- Internal annotation strip becomes two small labels or disappears.
- Product crops to the assignment context controls.

### Problem bridge

Desktop composition:

- A precise assignment timeline occupies the full content width.
- Left endpoint: assignment set.
- Center: hidden learning activity.
- Right endpoint: final response submitted.
- One concise paragraph beneath.
- No decorative illustrations.

Mobile composition:

- Vertical timeline.
- Keep all six middle-state details readable.

### Product walkthrough

Desktop composition:

- One stable product canvas on the right.
- Stage navigation on the left.
- Each stage changes the dominant placeholder shown in the canvas.
- For the static prototype, display all three stage states vertically or through clearly separate sections.
- Do not require JavaScript tabs to understand the page.
- Copy is practical and short.
- The instructor signals stage ends with examples of actions:
  office hours, targeted practice, review session, next assignment.

Mobile composition:

- All stages are visible in sequence.
- No tabs, carousels, or accordions for core content.
- Actions appear after the signals frame.

### Why MyTA and institutional fit

Desktop composition:

- Headline and one comparison statement.
- Four positioning points in a text-led grid with no enclosing cards.
- A contained pale-blue adoption block below:
  “Start with one course. Review the fit before scaling.”
- Include the LMS-companion statement as text, not logos.

Mobile composition:

- Positioning points stack.
- Adoption block follows immediately.

### Pilot CTA

- Direct course-oriented invitation.
- Include a compact three-item expectation:
  1. Share one assignment
  2. Review the walkthrough
  3. Discuss a focused pilot
- Present as one sentence or inline sequence, not cards.
- Primary and secondary actions remain unchanged.

### Persona risk checks

Reject this concept if:

1. It feels like an LMS feature page.
2. It becomes too operational and loses MyTA’s vision.
3. The timeline resembles student surveillance.
4. The adoption block implies completed integrations.
5. The page looks interchangeable with Nectir or Canvas.

## 6. Cross-concept differences

| Decision | Concept A | Concept B | Concept C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary structure | Connected path | Editorial spreads | Precise workflow index |
| Hero | Split with connected product | Large product below statement | Split with annotated workspace |
| Walkthrough | Sticky or sequential path | Alternating full-width spreads | Stable workflow canvas |
| Tone | Connected and product-led | Academic and editorial | Practical and precise |
| Main risk | Decorative process diagram | Insufficient product immediacy | Generic LMS utility |
| Primary reference | Granola | Google for Education | Class Companion |

## 7. Required prototype output

For each concept create:

1. A complete desktop prototype at 1440 pixels.
2. A tablet check at 1024 pixels.
3. A complete mobile prototype at 390 pixels.
4. One full-page desktop screenshot.
5. One full-page mobile screenshot.
6. A short evidence map listing the references used for each section.
7. A self-audit against all eight persona gates.
8. A limitations note.

The self-audit is evidence for review, not approval.

Only the founder and external review process may approve a concept.
