# MyTA Website Art Direction Lock

## Objective

Create a credible higher education product website that leads qualified instructors toward a focused pilot conversation.

Primary action: Request a Pilot

Secondary action: Watch the Demo

## Core design idea

The assignment is the central object. The website reveals how course context, guided student work, and instructor attention move through it.

## Audience

1. University instructors
2. Teaching and learning leaders
3. University decision makers
4. IT, accessibility, privacy, and procurement reviewers

## Tone

1. Calm
2. Precise
3. Academic
4. Product confident
5. Instructor controlled
6. Truthful about current capability

Avoid futuristic excitement, surveillance framing, consumer AI language, and broad transformation claims.

## Brand

1. DM Sans only
2. Blue: `#2563EB`
3. Teal on dark: `#16A085`
4. Teal on light: `#14806F`
5. Cyan: `#38BDF8`
6. Navy: `#172033`
7. Ivory: `#FAFAF7`
8. Soft blue: `#EAF2FF`
9. White: `#FFFFFF`

## Approved page rhythm

1. White product authority Hero
2. Navy connected workflow story
3. Ivory focused pilot conversion
4. Compact footer

Do not repeat the same composition in consecutive sections.

## Reference roles

1. Linear: product scale, alignment, and interface authority
2. Granola: continuous workflow storytelling
3. Gradescope: reduced course change anxiety
4. Turnitin Clarity CTA: low pressure product conversion
5. Google for Education: education first credibility

Borrow structural principles only. Do not copy branding.

## Product truth

Use current MyTA screenshots and approved project statements. Clearly separate current product capability from future plans.

Never imply unsupported:

1. Customer adoption
2. Learning outcomes
3. Mastery measurement
4. Institution approval
5. LMS integration status
6. Security certification
7. Accessibility certification
8. Production readiness
9. Real time intervention
10. Assignment level analytics not shown in the product

## Anti generic design rules

Reject:

1. Three equal feature cards
2. Repeated rounded white containers
3. Gradient headline text
4. Glows, orbs, grids, stars, and neural network graphics
5. Abstract AI imagery
6. Fake dashboards
7. Floating interface fragments
8. Decorative timeline dots
9. Excessive pill controls
10. Generic alternating feature rows
11. Placeholder logos and testimonials
12. Unnecessary statistics
13. Copy that could describe any AI company
14. Mobile created by merely stacking desktop
15. Whitespace without a communication purpose

## Motion

Motion must explain progression or state. Keep it restrained, pausable, and compatible with reduced motion. No parallax, bounce, spring, or decorative motion.

## Responsive design

Desktop, tablet, and mobile are separate compositions sharing one system. Product evidence must remain readable. Do not shrink a complete desktop interface until it loses meaning.

## Approval rule

No section enters the combined homepage until its desktop and mobile visual evidence passes founder review.
